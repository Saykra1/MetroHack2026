# Prior art (short)

## (a) Similar competitions — what won and why
- **This hackathon (Moscow Transport Hackathon / LCT, 2026)**: public team repos exist ([Manticore-MT/tram-forecast](https://github.com/Manticore-MT/tram-forecast) — so far a stub with synthetic data, 3 horizons day/month/year, WAPE, manual weather/event coefficients; [teamv39/mt-hack_predictor](https://github.com/teamv39/mt-hack_predictor) — other track, CatBoost+SHAP). Nothing ahead of us methodologically. Organizer page: [mt-hackathon.ru](https://mt-hackathon.ru/), [i.moscow/lct](https://i.moscow/lct).
- **Kaggle M5 (Walmart, 28-day daily horizon)**: winners = LightGBM global models (tweedie/poisson loss), calendar + price + event features, several models per horizon/store, **non-recursive (direct) setups competitive with recursive**; simple exponential-smoothing/profile baselines were hard to beat at low aggregation. [M5 results paper (Makridakis et al., IJF 2022)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijforecast.2021.11.013).
- **Kaggle Rossmann (6-week daily sales)**: gradient boosting (XGBoost) with heavy feature engineering — day-of-week, days-to/since holiday, school holiday, promo, store-level target statistics; entity-embedding NN was 3rd. Lesson: **distance-to-holiday features and per-entity level statistics** carry most signal. [Rossmann](https://www.kaggle.com/c/rossmann-store-sales), [entity embeddings paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.06737).
- **Kaggle Corporacion Favorita (16-day horizon)**: LightGBM, one model per horizon day, features = rolling means over multiple windows by weekday; holiday handling crucial. [Favorita](https://www.kaggle.com/c/favorita-grocery-sales-forecasting).
- **Kaggle Web Traffic (60-day horizon)**: seq2seq RNN won, but **median-of-medians by weekday** baseline was within a few % — seasonal profiles are strong for long horizons without fresh lags. [Web Traffic](https://www.kaggle.com/c/web-traffic-time-series-forecasting).
- **Transit ridership literature**: smart-card (AFC) hourly forecasting — best long-horizon results from profile/"historical average by day-type x hour" + ML residual; deep models only win on short horizons with recent lags. Weather effects on ridership are small (typically -2..-10% on heavy rain/snow days, larger on weekends/leisure trips). Survey: [Liu et al., Transportation Research Part C / "Short-term passenger flow prediction" reviews]; weather: [Arana, Cabezudo, Penalba 2014, TR-A](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tra.2013.10.019), [Singhal, Kamga, Yazici 2014, TR-A](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tra.2014.08.018).

## (b) Proven recipe for 61-day hourly forecast without recent lags
1. **Target decomposition**: y(route,date,hour) = Level(route, month) x DayShare(route, daytype) x HourShare(route, daytype, hour). Daytypes: Mon, Tue–Thu, Fri, Sat, Sun/holiday, pre-holiday, special (1 Nov working Sat, 29–30 Dec, 31 Dec).
2. **Level**: last clean 4–8 weeks (Sep–Oct, excluding disruption days) x seasonal multiplier (Nov/Oct, Dec/Oct — see (c)); robust median, not mean.
3. **LightGBM direct model** (one global model, log1p target or ratio-to-profile target, tweedie objective) with features: route, hour, dow, daytype flags, days-to/from holiday, school holiday, month/day-of-year, temperature, precipitation, snowfall, hour x daytype interactions, route-level target encodings from training. No lags shorter than the horizon (or only "same-weekday mean of last 4 weeks before cutoff" as a static level feature).
4. **Validation**: mimic the gap — train Jan–Aug, forecast Sep–Oct (61 days); WAPE on route x date x hour. Also check a "last 8 weeks" holdout.
5. **Holidays**: use holiday profile from 12–13 Jun / 2 May / 8–9 May for 3–4 Nov; blend weekday/Saturday for 1 Nov; separate curve for 29–31 Dec (heavily damped mornings).
6. **Structural breaks**: apply known route events manually (route 5 from 16 Dec, 7/50 weekend repairs to ~30 Nov, T1 from 13 Nov) — ML can't learn them from Jan–Oct.
7. **Reconciliation**: forecast at route x hour, then scale hours so they sum to a separately forecast daily total (daily model is more stable); WAPE is dominated by big routes/peak hours, so tune on those.
8. **Blend** profile model + LightGBM (weights from holdout); typically 3–10% WAPE gain over either alone.

## (c) Seasonality Nov/Dec vs Sep/Oct (Moscow)
- No monthly tram ridership series found in open data; data.mos.ru has only metro station quarterly flows ([dataset](https://data.mos.ru/opendata/7704786030-passajiropotok-po-stantsiyam-moskovskogo-metropolitena)); DepTrans press stats: [transport.mos.ru data](https://transport.mos.ru/mostrans/for_journs/data), Mosstat transport: [77.rosstat.gov.ru](https://77.rosstat.gov.ru/folder/162474).
- Stylized facts for Moscow public transport (DepTrans press releases on record days, which fall in **Oct–Dec**, typically mid/late December before New Year): Sep–Dec is the high season; **Nov ≈ Oct (±3%)**, **1–25 Dec ≈ Oct to +5%** on working days (shopping, corporate events, darker/colder -> fewer cars/bikes/walking), then **29–30 Dec drop to ~60–75% of normal weekday**, **31 Dec ~40–50%**, early-Jan holiday ~30–50%. No tram-specific evidence found; assume the same pattern as the network.
- Weekend share rises in December (pre-holiday shopping Saturdays), evening hours (17–21) grow relative to morning peak in the last two weeks of December.
- Practical recommendation: set Nov multiplier = 1.00 x Oct (clean weeks), Dec 1–26 = 1.00–1.03 x Oct, then special daytype curves for 27–31 Dec. The Dec multiplier cannot be tuned (no December in training), so keep it close to 1 and rely on calendar curves.
- Caveat: these ratios are expert priors, not measured from open data; validate direction using Jan–Oct 2025 month-to-month levels (Sep->Oct change) in the provided data.
